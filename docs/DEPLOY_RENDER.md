# Put the app online for free (Render)

This guide puts the app on the internet using **Render's free plan**. Free plans are meant for testing, so read "Limits of the free setup" at the end. Render's free-plan facts here come from Render's documentation (checked 2026-10-01).

The repository contains a setup file (`render.yaml`). Render reads it and creates everything in one go:

- the app (website and background worker together);
- a database (Postgres);
- a Redis store.

You need three accounts, all free: **GitHub** (you already have it), **Brevo** (to send emails) and **Render** (hosting).

## Step 1: Email sending with Brevo (about 10 minutes)

The app sends emails, for example "verify your email" after sign-up.

1. Sign up at https://www.brevo.com (free plan).
2. Add and verify a **sender**: in Brevo go to **Senders, Domains & Dedicated IPs > Senders** and add the email address emails should come from (for example your Gmail address). Click the confirmation link Brevo emails you.
3. Open **SMTP & API** (in the account menu, top right) and the **SMTP** tab. Note:
   - **SMTP server** (usually `smtp-relay.brevo.com`)
   - **Port** (use `587`)
   - **Login** (looks like `xxxxxx@smtp-brevo.com`)
   - Click **Generate a new SMTP key** and copy the key. It is shown only once.

Keep these values for step 2. If Brevo's screens show different values, use what Brevo shows.

## Step 2: Create the app on Render (about 15 minutes, mostly waiting)

1. Go to https://render.com and sign up with **GitHub**. Allow Render to access the `WhatsApp-SaaS` repository.
2. In the Render dashboard click **New > Blueprint**.
3. Choose the `WhatsApp-SaaS` repository. For **branch**, choose `claude/wizardly-rubin-d37kv6` (the latest work is there).
4. Render shows what it will create: `whatsflow` (web service), `whatsflow-db` (Postgres) and `whatsflow-redis` (Key Value), all on the **Free** plan.
5. Render asks for the values it cannot fill in itself:

   | Setting                                                                                 | What to enter                                  |
   | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
   | `SMTP_HOST`                                                                             | Brevo SMTP server, e.g. `smtp-relay.brevo.com` |
   | `SMTP_USER`                                                                             | Brevo SMTP login                               |
   | `SMTP_PASSWORD`                                                                         | the Brevo SMTP key                             |
   | `EMAIL_FROM`                                                                            | `WhatsFlow <the-sender-email-you-verified>`    |
   | `WHATSAPP_APP_ID`, `WHATSAPP_APP_SECRET`, `WHATSAPP_CONFIG_ID`, `WHATSAPP_VERIFY_TOKEN` | **leave empty for now** (step 4)               |

   Secrets such as the login secret and the encryption key are generated by Render automatically. You never see or type them.

6. Click **Apply**. The first build takes several minutes. When the `whatsflow` service shows **Live**, its address appears at the top, for example `https://whatsflow-abcd.onrender.com`. That is your app.

If the build or start fails, open the service's **Logs** tab and send me the last 30 lines.

## Step 3: First login

1. Open your app address and click **Sign up**. Create your account and workspace.
2. Check your email (and the spam folder) for the verification email and click the link.

If no email arrives, recheck the four email settings in Render (service > **Environment**) and that the sender is verified in Brevo.

## Step 4: Connect Meta (WhatsApp)

Follow `docs/META_SETUP_GUIDE.md`, using your Render address wherever the guide says `https://YOUR-APP-ADDRESS`:

- **Allowed domains** and **Valid OAuth redirect URIs**: `https://whatsflow-abcd.onrender.com`
- **Webhook callback URL**: `https://whatsflow-abcd.onrender.com/api/webhooks/meta`

Then, in Render, open the `whatsflow` service > **Environment**, fill in the four `WHATSAPP_*` values, and save. Render restarts the app with them, which takes a few minutes.

Order matters for the webhook: Meta's **Verify and save** button only succeeds after the app is running with the same `WHATSAPP_VERIFY_TOKEN`. So save the four values in Render first, wait for **Live**, then click **Verify and save** in Meta.

## Step 5: Test checklist

1. **Settings > WhatsApp > Connect with Facebook**: finish Meta's window. The number should show **Connected**.
2. **Templates > Sync from WhatsApp**: your existing templates appear. If there are none, create one under **Templates > New template** (Utility) and wait for Meta's approval.
3. **Contacts > Add contact**: add your own personal WhatsApp number, with opt-in "Opted in".
4. Open the contact, click **Send WhatsApp message**, and send the template. It should arrive on your phone.
5. Reply from your phone. Within a few seconds the reply appears in **Inbox**, and you can answer with free text (you are inside the 24-hour window).
6. Optional: create a small **Campaign** to a list containing only yourself.
7. For Meta's App Review you can record steps 4 and 5 (sending a message) and **Templates > New template** (creating a template).

## Limits of the free setup

These come from Render's free plan:

- **The app sleeps after 15 minutes without visitors.** The next visit takes about a minute to wake it up. A WhatsApp message arriving while it sleeps wakes it too; Meta re-sends webhooks it could not deliver, so messages arrive late but are not lost.
- **Scheduled campaigns do not start while the app sleeps.** While a campaign is sending, keep its page open: the page refreshes every few seconds, which keeps the app awake.
- **The free database is deleted 30 days after creation.** Fine for testing. Before then we move to a paid plan or a free database elsewhere (for example Neon).
- **Redis keeps data in memory only.** After a restart the app recovers its queued work from the database within a few minutes.
- **Files are not kept.** Photos and documents customers send, and files you upload, are lost when the app restarts or redeploys. Persistent storage would need S3-compatible storage (for example Cloudflare R2) or a paid disk.
- **Small computer**: 0.1 CPU and 512 MB memory, so pages are slower than on a paid plan. If the logs show the app running out of memory, the cheapest paid web plan fixes it.

For real customers, move to paid plans (about 7 USD per month for the app, plus the database) or another host. Your data and settings can be moved.
